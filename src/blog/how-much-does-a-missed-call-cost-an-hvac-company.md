## The phone is still the front door

When an AC dies in July or a furnace quits in January, homeowners don't fill out a form and wait. They call, and if nobody picks up, they call the next company on the list. Urgent work like no cooling, no heat, breakdowns and replacements still comes in mostly by phone.

You can't put a fair price on that until you know how many calls you're actually missing. So start there.

## Count your missed calls for one week

Most phone systems and carriers keep a call log. Pull one normal week and count:

1. **Calls nobody answered live.** Include the ones that went to voicemail.
2. **Calls you got back to too late.** If the customer had already booked someone else, it counts.
3. **Web forms and texts that waited more than an hour.** Those are missed calls too, just quieter.

Leave out vendors, spam and wrong numbers. Then look at when the misses happened. They usually bunch up in evenings and weekends, at lunch, during heat waves and cold snaps, and whenever your techs are out and nobody's at the desk.

## What a missed call actually costs

A single missed call can carry four kinds of value:

1. **The service call itself:** the diagnostic and repair you would have run.
2. **The booked-job value:** the job value times the chance you'd have booked it.
3. **A replacement sale:** the occasional new system hiding inside a repair call.
4. **Future value:** the maintenance plan, the next tune-up, the referral.

## The formula

Missed calls × share that were real sales calls × share you'd have booked × average job value = revenue lost. Multiply that by your gross margin to get lost profit.

Here it is with made-up example inputs. Swap in your own.

| Step (example inputs) | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Missed calls a month | 20 |
| 70% are real sales opportunities | 14 calls |
| 60% would have booked if answered | about 8 jobs |
| 7 service jobs × $450 | $3,150 |
| 1 replacement × $7,000 | $7,000 |
| Revenue lost a month | about $10,150 |
| Profit lost at a 35% gross margin | about $3,550 |

Two numbers, two meanings:

- **Revenue lost:** about $10,150 a month in this example. That's work that went to someone else.
- **Profit lost:** about $3,550 a month. That's what actually leaves your pocket.

The point isn't the example. It's that once you put your own counts in, the number tends to be bigger than people expect, even when you're careful with it.

## Emergency calls are different

Count emergency calls on their own line. A no-heat call in a cold snap or a dead system in a heat wave books at a higher rate and often leads to a bigger ticket or a replacement. ## Questions

### What counts as a missed call?

Any call from a customer or prospect that nobody answers live, or that's answered too late to win the job. Leave out vendors, spam and wrong numbers.

### Does a call that goes to voicemail count as missed?

For revenue, mostly yes. Plenty of homeowners with a broken AC or furnace won't leave a message. They call the next company. Count voicemail as missed unless you reliably call back within minutes.

### Is the number revenue or profit?

The formula gives you revenue, the work that went elsewhere. Multiply it by your gross margin for profit. Both are worth knowing, but profit is what pays for any fix.
