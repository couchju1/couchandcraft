## Emergency triage: a decision tree, not "take a message"

A tool that takes a message and sends you a notification treats a gas smell and a quote request the same way. Trade-specific triage asks the question that decides what happens next. Here's a simplified HVAC example, not my full script:

1. Do you smell gas, or see or hear anything unsafe right now? If yes, tell the caller to leave the building and call the gas utility or 911, then alert the on-call tech.
2. Is there no heat, and is anyone in the home elderly, an infant or medically vulnerable? If yes, treat it as priority and route it to dispatch.
3. Is the system under warranty or a maintenance plan? Flag it before booking.
4. Is the address inside your service area? If not, decline politely.
5. Otherwise, offer the next open slot and book it.

The same approach covers an active leak, a sparking panel or a roof that needs a tarp after a storm. The Emergency Call Triage Scripts Pack in the [shop](/shop/) covers all four trades.

## What a website scan won't pick up

Some tools learn from your website and Google Business Profile. That helps, but the details that protect your margin are rarely written down there. These go into the rules:

- **HVAC:** system type and age, warranty status, owner or tenant.
- **Plumbing:** active leak or backup, access to the shutoff, home or business.
- **Electrical:** sparking, burning smell, tripped breaker and other unsafe conditions.
- **Roofing:** storm damage, tarp needed now, insurance claim in progress.
- **Every trade:** service area, after-hours dispatch rules and the commercial work you don't take.

## Where Goodcall or Rosie is the better fit

- You're a solo operator and want the lowest monthly cost.
- You're comfortable writing and testing your own call logic.
- You want a fast trial. Goodcall offers a free trial and Rosie has no setup fee.
- Basic message taking and spam filtering are enough for your volume.

## Questions

### Goodcall and Rosie start around $49 to $59 a month. Why pay more?

On sticker price they're cheaper, and I won't pretend otherwise. The real questions are what one missed job costs you, and who builds, tests and maintains the call logic. With a self-serve tool, that's you. With Lead Response, it's me.

### Rosie has no setup fee. Isn't setup free?

There's no fee, but you still set the service area, escalation rules, calendar and edge cases, then test them. That takes your time. I handle it from an intake form and a kickoff call.

### Goodcall connects to CRMs and field-service tools. Do you?

Connections matter, but they don't write your triage logic. I build the emergency and qualification rules and connect booking to your calendar. Tell me your tools and I'll confirm what connects before you commit.

### Do minute caps matter?

They can. Rosie prices by included minutes, so a busy season can push you into a higher tier. Goodcall prices by unique callers. Check the current limits on each pricing page.
