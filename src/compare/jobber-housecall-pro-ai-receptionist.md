## What you still set up yourself

With a built-in receptionist, you write the rules. Expect to write and keep up:

- Which situations count as emergencies, and who gets called.
- Your service area, and what to say to people outside it.
- Diagnostic fees and how to quote them.
- How membership and maintenance plans are handled.
- Warranty work, and when not to promise a slot.
- Availability and booking logic, updated as your schedule changes.

That's a few evenings of work up front, then ongoing upkeep. With Lead Response, I do the setup and tuning.

## Trade by trade

- **HVAC:** repair or replacement, system type and age, warranty status. A no-heat call with a vulnerable person at home gets priority.
- **Plumbing:** active leak or backup, access to the shutoff, home or business. Urgent hazards don't get offered a routine slot.
- **Electrical:** sparking, burning smell, tripped breaker. Unsafe conditions go to whoever's on call, not the calendar.
- **Roofing:** estimate or service call, storm damage, tarp needed now, service area and access.

These are examples, not my full scripts. The Emergency Call Triage Scripts Pack in the [shop](/shop/) covers all four trades if you'd rather build it yourself.

## When the built-in is enough

- Your call volume is low and you don't miss many.
- You only need simple scheduling.
- You like tuning rules and are happy to own that work.
- It's already in your plan and you want to try it first.

If that's you, use it. If you're losing jobs to after-hours calls, emergencies that need sorting or leads that go quiet, that's where a managed setup pays for itself.
